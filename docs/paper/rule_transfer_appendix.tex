\section{Rule-specific transfer of the frozen policies}
\label{app:rule-transfer}
Rule changes alter incentives as well as score scales. We therefore compare
win score against the same opponents within each rule, not raw points across
rules. Nintendo and Etohana document opening awards, seven-point doubling and
opponent-koi doubling, but differ in match length and repeated koi calls
\cite{nintendokoikoi,etohanakoikoi}. The profiles below are explicit modifications
of paper8, not reproductions of either publisher's complete rules.

All nine profiles retain eight rounds, 30 initial points, bankruptcy and
symmetric zero-sum settlement. Besides the unchanged control, we test:
six-point opening wins for four-of-a-month or four month-pairs; seven-point
doubling; doubling against a loser who called koi; doubling for a winner who
called koi; and settlement of both players' current yaku to the round winner.
A common combination adds opening wins, seven-point and opponent-koi doubling,
five-point sake yaku, no declaration-count bonus and scoreless exhausted hands.
Two further combinations add either automatic settlement on the next yaku
increase after a koi call, or both own-koi doubling and both-yaku settlement.
Opening awards are never multiplied. Exact precedence and tie rules are in
\path{docs/RULE_VARIANTS.rst}.

The two frozen parents and released opponents retain their original neural
weights and public feature encoder. All receive rule-correct public endgame
assistance. Fixed GPU batch arithmetic defines this experiment's agents;
the original strict-CPU final match rates are not its control. There is no
variant-specific training. The isolated Python and shared C++/CUDA engines
pass 21 focused tests, including full trajectory differential checks for all
nine profiles. Every experimental action trace is independently replayed
through Python before its result is accepted.

\subsection{Selection and first untouched confirmation}
Development uses 256 shared duplicate-deal streams per profile, both seats,
both parents and both opponents: 18,432 matches, 124,255 round endings and
1,829,605 decisions. Selection maximizes the worse parent's equal-opponent
mean win score. Both-yaku settlement is selected at 51.8066\%, compared with
50.8789\% for the control. This is a development ranking, not evidence that
the selected rule maximizes achievable AI strength.

The fixed selected-versus-control comparison then consumes 2,048 new streams:
32,768 matches, 253,249 round endings and 3,782,656 decisions. Both seats are
averaged within each seed, which couples rules and opponents. Twenty thousand
seed-cluster bootstrap replicates give a one-sided 97.5\% lower bound for each
parent mixture, a nominal 95\% Bonferroni family. These approximate sampling
bounds are distinct from exact conditional search certificates.
The family covers the two parent comparisons within a cohort, not every
sequential research decision in the project.
