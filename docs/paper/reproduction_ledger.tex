\section{Reproduction and evidence}
\label{app:reproduction}
Source code and experiment documentation are available at
\url{https://github.com/sheetau/koikoi}. The primary comparison uses the
published implementation at the revision identified in Reference~\cite{pinnedkoikoi}.
The completed adaptation
study and its earlier manuscript are preserved in a local evidence archive
from project snapshot \texttt{f5cf955}. The present manuscript revision adds
related work, completed PPO and paired-supervision comparisons, and the
subsequent fitting diagnostics, followed by the completed direct-response
and learner-return comparisons. It also adds the exact critic-conditioning
analysis and its CPU/CUDA verification, bound in
\path{docs/paper/generated/observer_evidence.json}. The finite-cutoff extension
and its exact fractions are bound in
\path{docs/paper/generated/observer_trace_evidence.json}.

The final-round adaptation and public-opponent identification results are
bound in \path{docs/paper/generated/public_revelation_evidence.json}. The
tail comparison adds 1,908,865 evaluation decisions, 127,476 round endings
and 16,384 complete matches on 2,048 fresh deals. Its 133,120 training tails
are conditional continuations, not complete training matches. Identification
reuses the training prefixes and performs 44,170 strict CPU predictions;
the independent audit adds no new policy inference or independent deals.

The public-memory comparison is bound in
\path{docs/paper/generated/public_memory_evidence.json}. Its four arms add
3,725,648 training decisions and 266,240 conditional final-round completions.
Evaluation adds 2,857,337 decisions, 191,262 round endings and 24,576 full
matches on 2,048 fresh deals. Public-model elimination makes 355,486 strict
predictions; independent replay checks 300,092 historical inputs and masks
without new policy inference. The ledger retains all 18 contrasts.

The multi-round comparison is bound in
\path{docs/paper/generated/public_continuation_evidence.json}. Its four arms
add 18,982,723 training decisions, 266,240 conditional match completions and
1,277,944 conditional round endings. Full-match evaluation adds 2,856,503
decisions, 191,108 round endings and 24,576 matches on 2,048 fresh deals.
Public-model checks add 201,568 strict predictions. Independent replay verifies
146,184 historical inputs and masks without new neural inference or independent
deals. Root reconstruction is accounted for separately in the ledger.

The renewed-chance comparison is bound in
\path{docs/paper/generated/renewed_continuation_evidence.json}. New training
adds 19,033,187 decisions, 266,240 conditional completions and 1,276,266
conditional round endings, using 133,120 distinct chance keys shared between
public and masked conditions. Evaluation adds 4,754,060 decisions, 318,260
round endings and 40,960 full matches on 2,048 fresh deals. Identification
uses 399,624 strict predictions. Independent replay checks 290,156 historical
inputs and masks and reproduces all 66 contrasts without new neural inference
or independent deals. All three replication criteria fail; the reserved final
confirmation remains unused.

The machine-readable evidence ledger,
\path{docs/paper/generated/evidence.json}, binds the completed studies'
protocols, checkpoints, raw outcomes, audits and numerical tables. It records
SHA256 digests for each source artifact.
The completed development runner is \path{scripts.run_cold_ppo_screen};
the held-out evaluation runner is \path{scripts.run_final_published_test}.
Their audit modules verify artifact identities, accounting and paired
statistics. Full trajectory reproduction uses the recorded runtime and pinned
comparator dependencies.

Development used deals 7,200,000--7,204,999, while the completed held-out
comparison used 100,000,000--100,009,999. Those held-out deals remain assigned
to the completed study. The new arbitration protocol records fresh training
and development ranges in \path{runs/arbitration_study/protocol.json}.
\path{docs/ARBITRATION.md} describes the arms, budgets and evaluation criteria.
\path{docs/paper/generated/arbitration_evidence.json} separately binds the
completed PPO extension's audit, raw paired outcomes and figure.
\path{docs/paper/generated/paired_evidence.json} binds the paired-supervision
table, recomputed raw match comparisons, datasets, fitted models and diagnostic
reports. The paired study uses development deals 22,000,000--22,000,999.
Its two datasets share 623,232 source decisions across all fitted controllers;
branch generation adds 15,406,127 decisions and 262,144 branch completions.
Evaluation adds 5,551,259 decisions. These are distinct computation categories.

The completed fixed-role hybrids and continuation-target diagnostics are bound
separately in \path{docs/paper/generated/followup_evidence.json}. Static changes
are recomputed from raw matches. The target-control results retain dataset,
feature-cache and checkpoint identities. The completed direct-response comparison is bound in
\path{docs/paper/generated/response_evidence.json}. It uses 34,078,720 training
decisions and 13,947,308 evaluation decisions, with all 120,000 evaluation
matches on 5,000 shared deals. The learner-return comparison is bound separately
in \path{docs/paper/generated/learner_evidence.json}. Its four new training runs
use 34,078,720 decisions, 2,274,630 round endings and 294,469 completed matches.
The two reused signed-response endpoints add a separate pre-existing dependency
of 17,039,360 training decisions. Evaluation uses 18,542,184 decisions across
160,000 matches on 5,000 shared deals, numbered 24,000,000--24,004,999.
The independent audit recomputes all 16 outcomes and 24 paired contrasts from
the raw seat-swapped records.

The fixed-observer ledger is \texttt{observer\_selfplay\_evidence.json}.
Its four new arms
use 34,078,720 decisions, 2,278,384 round endings, 292,470 completed matches and

The public-seat comparison is bound in \texttt{seat\_observer\_evidence.json}.
Four new arms use 34,078,720 decisions, 2,277,571 round endings,
292,388 completed matches and 68,157,440 value rows. The archived observer
endpoints supply 17,039,360 pre-existing training decisions. Evaluation uses
18,586,219 decisions across 160,000 matches on 5,000 shared deals, numbered
28,000,000--28,004,999. The independent audit reproduces all 16 outcomes and
24 paired contrasts, the matched warmup checks and the failed primary
conjunction. No endpoint is promoted and the reserved confirmation deals remain
unused.

The seat-label and actor-input audits are recorded in
\path{runs/observer_seat_information_v2/independent_audit.json} and
\path{runs/seat_feature_reconstruction/audit.json}. The latter checks 862
discard, 52 hand-capture, 28 draw-capture and 82 stop/continue observations,
adding no simulator decisions. The original seat-label collection used 512
decisions, 33 round endings and 4 completed matches; a first collection that
failed during JSON serialization adds 256 decisions, 17 round endings and
2 matches. The reported 1,024 probes come from the successful saved collection
and its relabelings, not from additional independent games.


The temporal checks are recorded in
\path{runs/observer_temporal_reconstruction/audit.json} and
\path{runs/observer_score_reconstruction/audit.json}. They verify all 3,764
same-round pairs against native earlier actor inputs and recorded actions:
3,259 discard, 286 hand-capture, 82 draw-capture and 137 continuation roots.
These pairs overlap within the retained trajectories. The input/action decoder
also rejects 512 requests for actions not yet taken and 512 different-round
requests. The caller enforces both match and round identity because displayed
round numbers repeat between matches. Both checks add zero simulator decisions,
round endings, completed matches, policy inferences and training updates.

The duration comparison trains on 131,727,360 decisions, including both short
prefixes once, and records 1,133,134 completed training matches. Evaluation uses
11,376,824 decisions, 762,598 round endings and 98,304 complete matches.
A Windows progress-file replacement error interrupted the first evaluation.
Eight complete cells were copied unchanged; four incomplete cells were rerun
on the identical schedule. All 5,753 preserved complete prefix rows match
repeated inference exactly. Those additional 667,309 recorded decisions and
44,637 round endings are duplicate work, not extra evidence; interrupted
unrecorded work is unknown. A separate recovery capability repeats eight
existing matches. Native CPU replay verifies all scheduled evaluation actions,
endpoints and work; separate scalar calculations reproduce the statistics.
The audit does not repeat training or neural inference. All 72 secondary
coordinates and four primary comparisons are in
\path{docs/PUBLISHED_DURATION_RESULTS.rst}; the source and table bindings are in
\path{docs/paper/generated/duration_evidence.json}.


The Point-initialization comparison uses 51,118,080 training decisions,
3,398,982 round endings, 439,369 completed matches and 3,120 updates, including
warmup once. Evaluation uses 15,229,803 decisions, 1,016,240 round endings and
131,072 complete matches. Independent native replay checks every recorded
action; separate scalar calculations reproduce all 120 secondary coordinates
and four simultaneous bands. Replay adds no independent matches or neural
inference. All results are in \path{docs/POINT_INITIALIZATION_RESULTS.rst};
bindings and table values are in
\path{docs/paper/generated/point_initialization_evidence.json}.

The four calibrated divisors are approximately 0.168081, 0.194835, 0.219496
and 0.235761 for discard, hand capture, draw capture and continuation;
the bound calibration report retains full precision. The source-group split uses identifier modulo four;
group remainder three is validation. Forced actions are excluded. The final
probability diagnostic reuses 3,258 validation rows with 3,045 unforced choices:
2,578 discard, 122 hand capture, 130 draw capture and 215 continuation rows.
Six endpoints require 18,270 singleton CPU calls; original WP/Point scores are
cached. Calibrated-versus-fixed greedy actions differ on 232/200 discard rows
and 3/6 continuation rows at the two seeds. The diagnostic adds no simulator
steps, matches or learning. Its independent scalar audit checks population,
probabilities and decisions from saved scores without repeating inference.

The auxiliary study's 51,118,080 PPO decisions cover 3,369,134 round endings
and 437,799 completed matches. Auxiliary collection adds 8,936,915 decisions,
589,918 round endings and 76,800 matches. Evaluation uses 15,240,199 decisions,
1,014,387 round endings and 131,072 complete matches. Independent action replay
and scalar statistics check all 120 secondary quantities and six simultaneous
bands. Full results and recovery accounting are in
\path{docs/SCORE_AUXILIARY_RESULTS.rst}; table and diagnostic bindings are in
\path{docs/paper/generated/score_auxiliary_evidence.json}.

A Windows DLL-directory registration failure required process-isolated recovery
of the auxiliary study. The failed prefix adds 2,703,360 PPO decisions; its 164
logged updates record 174,865 round endings and 22,736 matches. Round/match
counts for update 165 were not saved. Its 28 complete auxiliary collections
add 421,608 decisions, 27,488 round endings and 3,584 matches. Saved parameters,
optimizers, random states, numerical logs and collections agree with the
repeated prefix. This repeated work is additional cost, not an independent
replication.

The new arbitration experiments freeze the inherited actors. A traced
near-tie shows why this detail belongs to the inference specification: with
identical weights and observations, changing only parameter requires-grad
flags changes a stop/continue score by about $10^{-6}$ and breaks a tie.
The first parent's phase-only controller always selects its frozen parent
proposal, yet one of 1,000 Point pairs differs from the older deployed wrapper
in points; its aggregate win score is unchanged and mean points differ by 0.002.
Both learned controller arms share the frozen expert path. The diagnostic in
\path{docs/ARBITRATION.md} records the state and reproducible flag intervention.
