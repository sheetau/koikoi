\subsection{Outcome and margin supervision}
\label{sec:score-auxiliary}
We test whether predicting differences in final margin within the same match
outcome improves a policy trained to win. In our PPO implementation, the critic
has its own parameters, so its prediction loss does not train the actor's
Transformer encoders. We add auxiliary prediction directly to those encoders,
using the phase separation and policy-cloning penalty of PPG
\cite{cobbe2021phasic}. PPO continues to optimize terminal match outcomes.

\paragraph{Matched targets.}
Let $d$ be the terminal own-minus-opponent point margin and
$y=(\operatorname{clip}(d,-64,64)+64)/2$. Margins are even under the benchmark
rules, giving 65 labels. Let $q_j$ be the categorical prediction and
$G(y)=\{j:\operatorname{sign}(j-32)=\operatorname{sign}(y-32)\}$.
The two auxiliary objectives are
\begin{align}
 L_{\mathrm{outcome}}&=-\log\sum_{j\in G(y)}q_j,\\
 L_{\mathrm{margin}}&=-\log q_y
 =L_{\mathrm{outcome}}
  -\log\frac{q_y}{\sum_{j\in G(y)}q_j}.
\end{align}
Thus margin supervision adds the conditional likelihood within the observed
loss, draw or win group. Draws have a single label. Clipping preserves the
outcome group and merges extreme margins into the endpoint labels.

Both objectives use the same 65-output head and initialization, assigning equal
initial probability to loss, draw and win and uniform probability within each
nonzero group. Three linear heads operate on mean-pooled legal action-token
embeddings; hand and draw captures share an encoder and auxiliary head. The
heads add 50,115 training parameters and are discarded for play. Inputs contain
only public information and the acting player's cards. Final margins enter as
training labels. During auxiliary optimization, both conditions add
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\Vert\pi_\theta)$ on the sampled legal actions,
with coefficient one and score divisor 0.05. This comparison changes target
resolution and the gradients it induces while holding head size fixed.

\paragraph{Training comparison.}
For each of two seeds, we train three RL-Point-initialized conditions: ordinary
PPO, outcome supervision and margin supervision. They begin with identical
actor, critic, optimizer and environment states. Each runs 20 critic-only
warmup updates and 500 policy updates, totaling 8,519,680 PPO decisions. Every
five policy updates, each condition collects 128 complete self-play matches.
A reservoir retains one uniformly sampled observation for each visited
match/player/decision-phase stratum, including forced choices. The two auxiliary
conditions train for six epochs with 128-row minibatches; the control collects
the data without auxiliary optimization. The first collection is identical
within each seed, while later trajectories follow the resulting policies.

Auxiliary Adam uses learning rates $3\times10^{-6}$ for the actor and $10^{-4}$
for the added heads, $\epsilon=10^{-5}$ and gradient-norm clipping at 0.5.
The 100 auxiliary phases use 12,800 matches per condition. Table~\ref{tab:aux-work}
reports their actual work separately from PPO. Across all conditions, PPO uses
51,118,080 decisions in 437,799 completed matches; auxiliary collection uses
8,936,915 decisions in 76,800 matches. Requested match budgets are equal even
though trajectory lengths differ.

\paragraph{Playing-strength comparison.}
We evaluate all six endpoints and the two retained WP-initialized parents against
assisted RL-WP and RL-Point, using 4,096 shared deal streams per opponent in both
seats: 131,072 matches on 8,192 distinct streams. Greedy inference uses the
original action heads and public endgame assistance. The primary family contains
six differences in equal-opponent-mixture draw-adjusted win score: margin minus
control, outcome and retained parent at both seeds. The fixed criterion requires
all six simultaneous 95\% lower bounds to exceed zero. A 10,000-replicate
bootstrap resamples 4,096 compound blocks, preserving the shared WP/Point deal
indices, seats and models. All 120 secondary absolute and paired win/point quantities use
descriptive marginal intervals. The first-seed margin model is the candidate
specified before evaluation.

\paragraph{Completed fixed-state diagnostics.}
On 3,045 unforced inputs from the fixed 25-group validation corpus, the margin
and outcome models disagree on 331 actions (10.87\%) at seed 1 and 249 (8.18\%)
at seed 2. At score divisor 0.05, margin supervision gives lower mean entropy in
discard and both capture phases at both seeds; the continuation-phase difference
changes sign across seeds. These measurements establish changes on common
inputs. The complete-match comparison assesses whether they improve play.
