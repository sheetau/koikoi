\subsection{Evaluating population improvement}
\label{sec:uniform-evaluation}
The conditional-improvement identity supplies a population target when source
generation and the evaluated posterior agree. We implement a source that samples
each round independently and uniformly from valid deals, retaining the existing
initial-month rule. The learner's seat is assigned independently with probability
$1/2$. Earlier-round public identification supplies the fixed opponent, and
complete current-round replay supplies the hand posterior. Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-law}
derives the correspondence between this source and the planner's integer masses.

The evaluation has four populations: each of the two parents against each of
WP and Point, with 2,048 sources per population. Every source contributes one
$\Delta(H)$, including zero for missing roots and all parent fallbacks. Its mean
estimates the expected match-win improvement over that parent under the declared
law. Independent Python evaluation invokes the parent actor directly and executes
the candidate's complete program through all remaining draws; it checks both
values without importing native search action caches.

For inference, seven prespecified gain thresholds provide exact binomial lower
bounds on the frequency of improvements of different sizes. Their weighted sum
gives a lower bound on the population mean. We allocate a total error probability
of $0.05$ across all four populations and seven thresholds. Success requires
positive lower bounds in all four populations and a lower bound above
$0.1$ percentage point for each parent's equal WP/Point mixture. The sample
size, thresholds, policy and budgets were frozen before collection. This
8,192-source evaluation is in progress; the draft reports its design only.
